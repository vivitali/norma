## Discrepancies that change money materially

1. **The >$3M PTT bracket is modelled wrong in a way that overcharges mixed-use and undercharges nothing.**
   The code encodes a flat 4th bracket of **5%** on everything above $3,000,000. In law the 5% is not a
   bracket — it is the general **3%** rate *plus a separate further 2% levied only on the residential
   portion* of value above $3M. For a wholly residential Vancouver property the arithmetic is identical, so
   the current model is right for the common case. But it is structurally wrong: it cannot express a
   mixed-class property, and it hides the fact that BC has two stacked taxes. On a $4M home the two levies
   are $20,000 of "further tax" on top of $30,000 — this is not a rounding item. **Recommendation:** split
   into two `TransferLine`s so the further 2% is visible and separately labelled.

2. **`yoy` is off by 5.7 percentage points** — placeholder −0.005 vs. actual **−0.062** (MLS HPI composite,
   July 2026). Any projection, rent-vs-buy break-even, or appreciation model reading this field is being fed
   a flat market when the actual market fell 6.2% over the year. This is the single largest money-affecting
   error found.

3. **`propTax` is ~12% low** — 0.00297 vs. **0.00336394**. On the detached benchmark ($1.82M) that is roughly
   **$700/yr** of understated carrying cost, which flows straight into the "comfort ceiling" and inflates
   what the tool tells a buyer they can afford.

4. **`bench.condo` is ~$74k too high and `bench.house` ~$103k too low** — the placeholders predate the
   2025–26 correction. Condo: 762,000 → **688,000**. Detached: 1,720,000 → **1,822,900**.

5. **The newly-built-home exemption is entirely absent from the model.** It is a *separate* PTT programme
   (full exemption ≤ $1,100,000, phasing out to $1,150,000, effective 2024-04-01) and is **not** restricted to
   first-time buyers. For a Vancouver buyer of a new $1.05M condo this is up to **$18,500** of tax the tool
   currently tells them they owe and they do not. The `rebates` array holds only the FTHB `exemptBand`.
   Note the two programmes cannot be stacked — a buyer claims one.

6. **`fees.statusCert` at $60 is not a real Vancouver number.** BC caps the Form B certificate fee at $35 by
   regulation, but strata management firms bill document packages in the $150–$400 range. Whichever is
   intended, $60 matches neither. Small in absolute terms but it is the only strata-specific cost the model
   carries.

## Unverifiable / needs a human

- **`bench.newbuild` (1,090,000).** No public index publishes a new-build benchmark price. The placeholder
  is numerically almost identical to the July 2026 MLS HPI **composite** benchmark ($1,088,800), which
  strongly suggests it was copied from the wrong series. A human must decide what this field is meant to
  represent (new-build asking price? composite? townhouse?) before it can be sourced at all.
- **`rent` (3,150).** CMHC's Rental Market Report data tables were not reached directly. Neighbouring figures
  found: $2,900 average 2-bed *condo* rent and ~$3,170 average 2-bed *asking* rent (Q1 2025). Purpose-built
  2-bed average for the Vancouver CMA needs pulling from CMHC's Housing Market Information Portal.
- **`propTax` (0.00336394).** Confirmed only via a search snippet attributed to vancouver.ca, not by reading
  the rate table. Someone should open the City's 2026 tax-rate bylaw / rate page and read the Class 1 total.
- **`fees.moving` and `fees.setup`.** No authoritative source exists for either; these are judgement calls,
  not verifiable data. They should be labelled as user-adjustable assumptions in the UI rather than presented
  as jurisdiction facts.
- **`premiumTax = null`.** Correct per multiple industry sources, but not confirmed against a BC government
  PST exemption schedule. Low risk (BC unambiguously does not tax insurance premiums), but a primary cite
  would close it.
- **Interaction between FTHB and newly-built exemptions**, and whether the model should offer both, is a
  product decision, not a data question.
