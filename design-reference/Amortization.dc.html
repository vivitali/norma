7Amortization — built around the renewal cliff

Your Amortization tab, organised around the thing it warns about. Canadians do not lock a rate for the life of the loan: you take a five-year term and renew into whatever rate exists then, which makes renewal risk the largest unmodelled risk in any Canadian mortgage and the one that actually breaks budgets. So the renewal rate is the primary control here, not a footnote — move it and the schedule re-runs from the first renewal onward, recomputing the payment on the remaining balance over the remaining amortization exactly as a lender would, with the unstressed balance kept on the plot as a dotted line so the divergence is shown rather than asserted. The stacked bars are interest and principal per year, which is where the shape of a mortgage lives: at the start almost everything you pay is interest, and the year that flips is annotated on the plot. Term boundaries are marked, because those are the only moments the rate can change.
