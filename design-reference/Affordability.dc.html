2Affordability — the two numbers, and three levels of depth

The screen your README puts first, and the one the whole product exists for: the lender's ceiling is not your budget. Both numbers sit side by side at the same visual weight, and the ceiling is styled as a boundary — muted, labelled a ceiling, not a target — never as an achievement. Math is lifted verbatim from your Affordability tab, including the closed-form maximum price that lets property tax scale with price, and Canadian semi-annual compounding in the payment factor. Depth is a control, not a guess: the header switches between The answer (verdict, four tiles, three checks, gap bar), Why (each check reasoned out, the inputs that drive it, the monthly breakdown) and The math (stress-test derivation line by line, GDS and TDS gauges against their 39% and 44% limits, the comfort calculation). The jump rail beside it moves straight to any section, so advanced information is one tap away rather than buried. Enter a car payment and the impact chip tells you live what it costs you in borrowing capacity — on your example household that is the binding constraint, and almost nobody knows it.
