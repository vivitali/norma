3Scenarios — four down payments, three levels of depth, one reference column

Your Scenarios tab, with the depth idea pushed further. The answer is four cards and one recommendation — no table at all, because a table is not an answer. Compare brings up the full grid, and adds the mechanic that makes a wide comparison readable: tap any column header to make it the reference, and every other column switches from absolute figures to differences from it. Comparing 20% against 5% is the question people actually have, and reading it off two columns of absolutes is work. The math adds the derivation rows — loan-to-value, premium rate, contract rate, qualifying rate, stress-tested payment — plus the honest counterweight your notes insist on: above 20% each extra dollar earns exactly the mortgage rate, and since mortgage interest is not deductible in Canada, that rate is the after-tax hurdle. Two corrections to my earlier engine came from this tab: the legal minimum down payment now overrides a lower requested percentage, and closing costs are recomputed per column rather than held constant — in Ontario, Quebec or Saskatchewan the tax on the CMHC premium makes the 5% column cost more cash at closing than the table would otherwise show — while Manitoba, which dropped that tax in 2020, shows no such row at all. On phone the grid becomes a card carousel, never a horizontally-scrolling table.
