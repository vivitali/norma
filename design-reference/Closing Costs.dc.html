1Closing costs — the jurisdiction engine

Round one, because it is the screen that proves the location system. Everything below is driven by a jurisdiction record — no rule string is hard-coded in the view, so the bill is a renderer over a list of line items rather than a fixed set of rows. Change the location chip and watch line items appear, disappear and change their labels: Toronto stacks a municipal tax on the provincial one and taxes the CMHC premium in cash; Calgary has no transfer tax at all and shows a land-titles registration fee instead; Winnipeg has the tax but no first-time buyer rebate, stated plainly rather than omitted. Thirteen provinces and territories are selectable; the eight you picked carry verified city figures, the rest render exact provincial rules with a note over provincial-average local costs. The screen now follows the same disclosure model as 3a: cash needed on closing day is the hero figure, and the whole bill — the three fee groups, the credits, the cash check and the sources — sits as six uniform rows that open in place, on desktop and phone alike. One Expand all opens every line-item explanation and every bracket breakdown at once. Surface and type match too: Archivo throughout, bone and electric indigo, no card borders, semantic colour reduced to a dot. Manitoba and federal rule values are taken from the Reference Data tab of your Winnipeg model, dated 16 Aug 2026 — which caught a real error in my first pass: Manitoba removed PST on CMHC premiums in 2020, so that line no longer renders in Winnipeg at all. Figures outside Manitoba remain best-knowledge placeholders and are flagged verify-before-ship.
