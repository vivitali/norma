1Closing costs — the jurisdiction engine

Round one, because it is the screen that proves the location system. Everything below is driven by a jurisdiction record — no rule string is hard-coded in the view, so the closing-costs table is a renderer over a list of line items rather than a fixed set of rows. Change the location chip and watch line items appear, disappear and change their labels: Toronto stacks a municipal tax on the provincial one and taxes the CMHC premium in cash; Calgary has no transfer tax at all and shows a land-titles registration fee instead; Winnipeg has the tax but no first-time buyer rebate, stated plainly rather than omitted. Thirteen provinces and territories are selectable; the eight you picked carry verified city figures, the rest render exact provincial rules with a banner over provincial-average local costs. Behaviour is Radix-shaped (roving focus, ARIA-labelled sliders, in-place disclosure, nothing behind a modal) on a token layer that owes shadcn nothing visually — IBM Plex Sans and Mono, 2–3px radii, warm off-white, one slate-blue accent, semantic colour reserved for state. Manitoba and federal rule values are now taken from the Reference Data tab of your Winnipeg model, dated 16 Aug 2026 — which caught a real error in my first pass: Manitoba removed PST on CMHC premiums in 2020, so that line no longer renders in Winnipeg at all. The model's Closing Costs tab also contributed the cash check at the foot of the screen: available funds against net cash at closing, with surplus, shortfall and months of saving to close the gap. Figures outside Manitoba remain best-knowledge placeholders and are flagged verify-before-ship.
