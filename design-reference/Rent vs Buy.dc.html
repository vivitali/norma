4Rent vs Buy — 40 years, crossover annotated on the plot

Extended from your 15-year table to 40, which forced a real change to the model: the mortgage gets discharged part-way through. The schedule now runs monthly rather than annually, so at year 25 or 30 the payment stops, owner outlay drops by the full principal-and-interest amount, and the buy line visibly steepens. A 15-year horizon never had to model that. The payoff year is marked on the chart for exactly that reason. Everything else is your tab: capital deployed up front is the fair starting point for both paths — the buyer sinks it into the house, the renter invests it — and the two switches are yours, investing the monthly difference (on by default, the factor most calculators quietly omit) and appreciation (off runs the flat-market test). The crossover year is annotated directly on the plot rather than living in a caption, because that number is the answer. Depth 2 adds the verdict by holding period and the switch explanations; depth 3 adds the full 40-row schedule and both lists of what the model does not capture — the ones that favour buying and the ones that favour renting.
